Future Career

Three roles a Computer Engineering degree prepares you for
career 1

Computer Hardware Engineer

Hardware Engineers conduct research, design, develop and test Computer Components, such as Processors, Circuit Boards, Memory Devices and Routers. As of today many Hardware Engineers design the computing systems embedded in Cars, Home Appliances and Medical Equipment that connect to the Internet. Since Hardware Engineers work closely with Software Development Teams, a Strong Background in Programming is usually required along with Skills related to Designing Hardware. Typically a Bachelor's Degree in either Computer Engineering or Electrical Engineering is Required for these Positions.

Software Developer / Systems Engineer

Software Developers and Systems Engineers are responsible for developing the software that Computers, Phones and Other Devices Run On. Systems Software Developers specifically develop Operating System and Low-Level Software that supports Device Functionality including Smartphones, Car Systems and Industrial Equipment. Therefore Developers with a Computer Engineer Background are Extremely Valuable since they Understand How Software Interacts with Hardware. They are therefore Effective at Developing High Performance Reliable Systems. This Role Leads to Technical Leadership and Project Management Roles Over Time.

career 3

Embedded Systems Engineer

Embedded Engineers write software that is executed directly on Dedicated Hardware Platforms. Examples include Automotive Braking Systems and Smart Watches to Industrial Robots and Medical Monitors. Unlike Application Developers, Embedded Engineers Must Have an Understanding of the underlying Hardware Platform. Embedded Engineers Program in Low Level Languages such as C/C++. These programmers must meet Strict Constraints related to Memory Usage, Power Consumption and Real-Time Performance. Therefore Embedded Engineering is One of the Most Logical Career Paths for a Graduate of a Computer Engineering Program due to its requirement of Simultaneous Fluency in Both Hardware Design and Software Development.

Emerging Technology

Neuromorphic Computing. Brain-inspired chips for the AI era

What is neuromorphic computing?

Neuromorphic Computing is a Technology that Designs Computer Chips to Operate More Like the Human Brain by Using Artificial Neurons and Synapses Instead of Traditional Transistors and Clock Cycles. Instead of executing Instructions Sequentially, Neuromorphic Chips execute Information in Parallel Event Driven Bursts Making Them Much More Efficient for Perception/Pattern Recognition Tasks Than Conventional Processors.

How is it used today?

Both Intel (Loihi 2) and BrainChip (Akida) have commercially Released Neuromorphic Chips. Autonomous Vehicles utilize Neuromorphic Vision Processors to Recognize Objects in Real Time with much Less Power Consumed than a GPU. Additionally the Technology is Being Used in Cybersecurity Threat Detection, Healthcare Patient Monitoring, Aerospace Systems, etc... Any Edge-AI Device Where Battery Life and/or Speed are Critical Constraints.

Market size and growth

The neuromorphic computing market has been estimated to reach approximately $8-$9.5 billion in 2025, and is expected to reach $47-$59 billion in 2033. Venture Capital Funding for Neuromorphic Startups in 2025 exceeded over $200 Million. All major players (Intel, IBM, Samsung, & Qualcomm) have committed significant resources to developing the technology and positioning themselves within this emerging area.

How does this connect to Computer Engineering?

Computer Engineers develop the necessary skills to design neuromorphic hardware; including chip architecture, digital logic, hardware/software co-design and low level systems programming. As AI continues to become integrated into a variety of products (cars, wearables, robots, etc.) as well as being used to create implantable medical devices, those who can design efficient, brain inspired hardware will be some of the most valuable engineers in their respective industries.

Why this technology interests me

Neuromorphic Computing exists at the exact intersection of hardware and AI. This is precisely the area of focus I wish to pursue my professional career.
